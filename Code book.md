## Coding method for the SLR
### The coding process was a two-stage qualitative analysis, followed by an analysis of relationships.

Coding procedure

Identification of mechanisms (RQ1): Sustainability implementation mechanisms were first extracted from the empirical findings of the included studies. These included practices, activities, organizational arrangements, tools, and external factors related to sustainability implementation. The initial extraction did not use predefined organizational-level categories.

Categorization of mechanisms (RQ2): The extracted mechanisms were compared according to their main purpose and organizational context. Similar mechanisms were grouped together, resulting in five organizational levels:
•	Institutional: External pressures and expectations, such as regulation, reporting obligations, customer requirements, and procurement criteria.
•	Governance: Organizational strategies, policies, priorities, responsibilities, management decisions, and resource allocation.
•	Process: Practices that integrate sustainability into software development, including requirements engineering, Scrum, code reviews, Agile, and DevOps workflows.
•	Behavioral: Practitioner knowledge, competencies, awareness, communication, collaboration, and individual initiatives.
•	Technical: Software tools, code optimization, energy monitoring, architectural choices, and technical sustainability practices.

Identification of cross-level pathways (RQ3): After categorization, the studies were examined for reported relationships between mechanisms at different levels. A pathway was coded when the findings connected a mechanism at one level to a mechanism at another. The reported direction and supporting evidence were recorded. The presence of mechanisms at multiple levels alone was not sufficient.

Classification of pathway evidence: Relationships were classified as Documented, Partial, or Proposed. This distinguished operational activities from practitioner reports, statistical associations, and suggested pathways.
